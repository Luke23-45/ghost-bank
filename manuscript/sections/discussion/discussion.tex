\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Uniform Herding obtains higher mean final accuracy and lower mean forgetting
than the faithful iCaRL and static-bank baselines in the evaluated protocol.
This is a complete-method result: the comparison includes differences in
refresh policy, training objective, readout, and retained candidate storage.
It does not estimate the effect of refresh alone.

\subsection{Evidence About the Proposed Configuration}
\label{subsec:what-drives-performance}

Within Uniform Herding, NME evaluation is strongly favored over head-logit
evaluation: the latter reduces accuracy by $10.46$ pp and increases forgetting
by $30.36$ pp.  The two variants share the training procedure, so this result
supports matching the readout to the selected-exemplar representation in this
protocol.

The herding-selection variant yields $2.39$ pp higher accuracy than random
selection; its $1.16$ pp lower mean forgetting is not statistically
distinguishable with three paired seeds.  Removing KD changes accuracy by
$-1.48$ pp but increases forgetting by $11.66$ pp, supporting a retention role
for distillation under the reported objective.  Replacing the cosine-margin
head with a linear head has a smaller effect over the tested configuration
($-0.79$ pp accuracy and $+0.12$ pp forgetting).  These comparisons describe
the proposed implementation; they do not establish universal component
effects.

\subsection{Resource and Storage Implications}
\label{subsec:resource-allocation}

Reducing the active budget from 2,000 to 500 lowers accuracy by $10.05$ pp and
raises forgetting by $13.26$ pp, whereas changing retrieval from 64 to 128
does not improve the mean metrics.  The active-budget sweep also changes the
candidate capacity because $\rho=3$.  Uniform Herding therefore trades
additional candidate storage and refresh computation for an active replay set
of size $M$; it is not a total-storage-matched comparison with iCaRL or the
static bank.  The observed resource pattern is conditional on this backbone,
data stream, and tested range.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

All experiments use CIFAR-100, one ten-task partition induced by split seed
13, one ResNet-18 backbone, and three training seeds.  They do not measure
sensitivity to class order, task granularity, datasets, or architectures.  The
active-budget and retrieval sweeps cover only the reported values, and the
implementation default $\rho=3$ was not independently swept.  A newly observed
class can also retain its full transient stream before its first rebuild, so
the boundary candidate bound does not describe all mid-task storage.

The iCaRL comparison does not isolate refresh from the objective, readout, and
storage differences between the complete protocols.  No matched experiment
holds objective, head, readout, active budget, candidate multiplier, data order,
and seed set fixed while changing only the refresh policy.  Finally, paired
supporting comparisons use three matched seeds and should not be treated as
high-power estimates of small effects.

The next experiment should use one common objective, head, readout, active
budget, candidate multiplier, data order, and seed set, then replace
arrival-time prefix truncation with refresh-all-class candidate reselection.
That design would support a refresh-specific claim.
