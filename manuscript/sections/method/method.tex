\section{Uniform Herding and Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:method}

We study class-incremental learning on CIFAR-100 under a bounded active
exemplar budget.  Training proceeds over tasks $t=0,\ldots,T-1$.  Each task
introduces a disjoint set of $C_{\mathrm{new}}$ classes, and evaluation after
task $t$ covers all classes observed so far without task identity.  In the
main experiments, $T=10$ and $C_{\mathrm{new}}=10$.  The CIFAR-100 classes are
permuted with split seed 13 and remapped to contiguous internal labels.  From
each training class, 30 images are held out for probing and 20 for validation;
these images are not inserted into replay storage.

\subsection{Model and Training}
\label{subsec:model-training}

The learner is a ResNet-18 feature extractor $f_\theta:\mathcal{X}\to
\mathbb{R}^{512}$ with base width 64 and no dropout.  The main configuration
uses a cosine-margin classifier.  For feature vector $f_\theta(x)$, classifier
weight $w_j$, scale $s$, margin $m$, and target $y$, its logits are
\[
z_j(x;y)=
\begin{cases}
s\left\langle \bar f_\theta(x),\bar w_j\right\rangle-sm, & j=y,\\
s\left\langle \bar f_\theta(x),\bar w_j\right\rangle, & j\neq y,
\end{cases}
\]
where $\bar u=u/\|u\|_2$.  We use $s=30$ and $m=0.35$.  When a task is
introduced, the classifier head is expanded by 10 rows.  After the first task,
the new rows are imprinted from normalized class-mean features before
optimization.

Training uses SGD with learning rate 0.1, momentum 0.9, weight decay
$5\times10^{-4}$, batch size 128, gradient clipping at 1.0, mixed precision,
and 70 configured epochs per task.  Training images use random crop with
padding 4 and horizontal flip; evaluation uses normalization only.  Reported
runs use seeds 1993, 2023, and 42.

\subsection{Active Allocation and Candidate Refresh}
\label{subsec:active-candidate-memory}

Uniform Herding separates the active replay set from the candidate pool used
to refresh it.  Let $\mathcal{C}_t$ be the classes observed after task $t$ and
let $C_t=|\mathcal{C}_t|$.  Given active budget $M$, the quota for the $i$-th
class identifier is
\[
q_{c_i}^{(t)}=
\left\lfloor\frac{M}{C_t}\right\rfloor
+\mathbf{1}\{i<M\bmod C_t\},
\qquad i=0,\ldots,C_t-1.
\]
For the reported runs, every class has enough candidates and the selected
sets therefore sum to $M$ at each completed task boundary.  In general, the
implementation selects at most $q_c^{(t)}$ items when a candidate pool is
smaller than its quota.

At the start of task $t$, let $\mathcal{B}_c^{(t-1)}$ denote the persistent
candidate pool for an old class and let $\mathcal{P}_{c,\mathrm{cur}}^{(t)}$
denote the raw current-task stream for a newly observed class.  At the task
boundary, the candidate set for class $c$ is
\[
\mathcal{Q}_c^{(t)}=
\mathcal{B}_c^{(t-1)}\cup\mathcal{P}_{c,\mathrm{cur}}^{(t)},
\qquad
\mu_c^{(t)}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}_c^{(t)}|}
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_c^{(t)}}f_\theta(x).
\]
For a new class, the persistent pool is empty before its first rebuild.  The
current-task stream is retained until the boundary; after a class has a known
quota, its persistent candidate pool is maintained with class-local bounded
reservoir insertion.

Herding selects the active exemplars from $\mathcal{Q}_c^{(t)}$.  With
$S_0=0$, the $k$-th exemplar is
\[
x_k\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{x\in\mathcal{Q}_c^{(t)}\setminus
\{x_1,\ldots,x_{k-1}\}}
\left\|f_\theta(x)-\left(k\mu_c^{(t)}-S_{k-1}\right)\right\|_2^2,
\qquad S_k=S_{k-1}+f_\theta(x_k).
\]
The selected set is $\mathcal{E}_c^{(t)}=\{x_1,\ldots,x_{q_c^{(t)}}\}$, and
the NME prototype is
\[
\hat\mu_c^{(t)}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_c^{(t)}|}
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{E}_c^{(t)}}f_\theta(x).
\]
Uniform Herding applies this rebuild to every observed class after every task.
After selection, the persistent candidate pool retains the selected items and
bounded leftover candidates.  With the implementation default
$\rho=\texttt{pool\_multiplier}=3$, a completed boundary has at most $\rho
q_c^{(t)}$ candidates per class and at most $\rho M$ candidates in total in
the reported setting.  This bound excludes the transient full stream of a
class before its first rebuild.  Thus, $M$ is the active replay budget and
$\rho M$ is the boundary candidate-storage bound; the method does not have a
total storage budget of $M$.

The candidate pool is a bounded implementation buffer, not a claim of uniform
sampling over the entire historical stream.  Replay draws only from the
selected sets.  The current runner uses the implementation default $\rho=3$;
it does not expose this argument as an independently swept configuration.

\subsection{Replay, Distillation, and Prediction}
\label{subsec:replay-distillation}

For $t=0$, training uses the current-task minibatches.  For $t\ge1$, each
minibatch is augmented with $b$ selected exemplars sampled with replacement
from previously observed classes.  Sampling is uniform over stored exemplar
items rather than over classes, and retrieved raw images are re-augmented
before concatenation.  The main configuration uses $M=2{,}000$ and $b=64$.

Uniform Herding optimizes cross-entropy on the expanded label space together
with distillation on old classes.  A teacher is copied immediately before the
head is expanded.  For $t\ge1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_t
&=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(z(x;y),y)
+\lambda T_{\mathrm{KD}}^2
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(p^{\mathrm{teach}}(x)\,\middle\|\,p(x)\right),\\
p^{\mathrm{teach}}(x)
&=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
z^{\mathrm{teach}}_{0:C_{t-1}-1}(x)/T_{\mathrm{KD}}\right),\\
p(x)
&=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
\tilde z_{0:C_{t-1}-1}(x;y)/T_{\mathrm{KD}}\right),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\tilde z$ is the student's logit vector after undoing the
target-dependent cosine margin for the KD comparison.  The reported setting
uses $\lambda=1$ and $T_{\mathrm{KD}}=2$; the distillation term is absent for
$t=0$.

At evaluation, Uniform Herding uses nearest-mean exemplar prediction.  Given
the final representation, the prediction is
\[
\hat y(x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{T-1}}
\left\|\bar f_\theta(x)-\bar{\hat\mu}_c^{(T-1)}\right\|_2.
\]
The head-logit rule is used only for the designated ablation and for the
static-bank baseline's native protocol.

\subsection{Baselines and Supporting Analyses}
\label{subsec:baselines-supporting}

The faithful iCaRL baseline herds each new class once from its full current-task
pool, stores the prioritized order, and later keeps the prefix required by the
new quota.  It does not re-herd old classes.  Its NME means are recomputed in
the current feature space over the retained exemplars, and its transient
current-task pool is discarded at the task boundary.  Training uses the
iCaRL-style sigmoid binary cross-entropy targets for old classes.

The static-bank baseline uses random replacement within class pools, the same
nominal active budget and retrieval count, and cross-entropy training with
head-logit prediction.  At task boundaries, its persistent active store is
capped at the nominal budget, unlike Uniform Herding's additional candidate
pool.

The supporting Uniform Herding comparisons change one designated choice at a
time: KD is removed, NME is replaced with head-logit prediction, the
cosine-margin head is replaced with a linear head, or herding is replaced with
random selection.  Resource sensitivity varies $M\in\{500,2000,4000\}$ and
$b\in\{32,64,128\}$ around the main configuration.  T1 is a comparison of
complete protocols; it matches the nominal active and retrieval budgets but
not total retained storage, training objective, or readout across all rows.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{subsec:evaluation-metrics}

After each task $i$, the model is evaluated separately on every task
$j\le i$, yielding an accuracy matrix $R$.  Final average accuracy is
\[
\mathrm{ACC}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}R_{T-1,j}.
\]
Forgetting is the mean drop from each old task's best observed accuracy to its
final accuracy:
\[
\mathrm{F}=\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j=0}^{T-2}
\left(\max_{i\in\{j,\ldots,T-1\}}R_{i,j}-R_{T-1,j}\right).
\]
Backward transfer is
\[
\mathrm{BWT}=\frac{1}{T-1}\sum_{j=0}^{T-2}
\left(R_{T-1,j}-R_{j,j}\right).
\]
All scalar metrics are reported as the mean and population standard deviation
over the three seeds.  For supporting and sensitivity rows, deltas are
computed per seed against the matched Uniform Herding run before averaging.
